## Introduction <!--block:lead-->

In modern industrial production and material handling, the **5 ton overhead crane** is one of the most widely used mid-capacity lifting machines, serving machinery manufacturing, warehousing and logistics, metal processing, mining, papermaking and many other industries. Combining **moderate lifting capacity, flexible configuration options and a mature, reliable structure**, it is the first choice for small-to-medium tonnage lifting scenarios.

Depending on the building structure, lifting headroom, duty cycle and control requirements, 5 ton overhead cranes come in many types: from the structurally simple LD single girder crane, to compact low-headroom and underhung designs, up to high-performance FEM European-standard cranes and double girder cranes — covering the needs of different industries and working conditions.

This article takes a deep dive into the various types of 5 ton overhead cranes, from single girder to double girder designs, analyzes the factors that drive their prices, and uses parameter comparisons and real project cases to show how to choose the best-fitting equipment. Whether you are an engineer, a purchasing manager or a facility manager, this guide will give you comprehensive decision support.

## Types <!--block:types-->

Below are the common types of 5 ton overhead cranes, covering both single girder and double girder designs for different industrial needs:

**LD single girder overhead crane** — an economical single girder crane equipped with an electric hoist, suitable for light and medium material handling. Features: simple structure, low cost, easy installation; ideal for standard workshops.

**Low headroom single girder overhead crane** — purpose-built for workshops with limited headroom; the hoist runs beside the girder to gain extra lifting height. Features: saves vertical space and lowers the required building height; ideal for space-constrained environments.

**Underhung (under-running) single girder overhead crane** — suspended from runway tracks fixed to the workshop roof, suitable for buildings without load-bearing columns but with a roof that can carry the load. Features: saves floor space; ideal for small plants or warehouses.

**FEM single girder overhead crane** — designed to the European FEM standard and fitted with a low-headroom electric hoist for high-precision tasks. Features: lightweight, high rigidity, energy-saving; ideal for demanding workshops.

**Offset-trolley single girder overhead crane** — the hoist trolley is offset to one side of the main girder, optimizing lifting height and space utilization. Features: suits low-headroom and special-layout workshops; smooth travelling.

**Monorail crane** — runs along a single rail for straight-line or curved material handling. Features: compact structure; ideal for narrow spaces or dedicated production lines.

**European top running double girder overhead crane (wire rope hoist)** — European-standard double girder design carrying a hoist trolley or winch trolley. Features: compact structure, high efficiency, variable frequency speed control; ideal for high-end industrial applications.

**LH type double girder overhead crane (hoist trolley)** — double girder design with a hoist trolley, suitable for medium-duty load handling. Features: light structure and low profile; used in factories, mines and similar sites.

**QD type double girder overhead crane (winch trolley)** — equipped with a winch trolley for heavy-duty, high-frequency operation. Features: high load capacity and strong stability; ideal for steel mills, mines and other heavy industries.

## Applications <!--block:apps-->

Thanks to its flexible structure, moderate capacity and easy operation, the 5 ton overhead crane plays a key role across many industries, handling daily transfer, precision assembly and high-frequency lifting alike. Typical application industries include:

- **Manufacturing**: moving and positioning raw materials, semi-finished products and components to keep automated lines running efficiently — common in automotive, home appliance and heavy equipment plants.
- **Warehousing & logistics**: precise stacking and retrieval of goods in tight or high-rack spaces, improving space utilization and loading/unloading efficiency in mid-size warehouses and distribution centers.
- **Machining workshops**: clamping, assembling and loading/unloading heavy workpieces on machine tools, improving operating safety and production accuracy, often alongside CNC equipment.
- **Construction & steel structure industry**: hoisting formwork, steel plates and components in rebar processing areas, precast plants and construction sites to speed up the work.
- **Petrochemical industry**: lifting, testing and maintaining large valves, piping and equipment, often in explosion-proof or corrosion-resistant configurations.
- **Papermaking & printing**: high-frequency lifting for paper roll handling and die-cut changeovers — especially suitable for moving master rolls or parts under 5 tons.
- **Energy & power industry**: supporting the assembly and hoisting of transformers, circuit breakers, busbars and other equipment in power plants and substation maintenance scenarios.

## Price factors <!--block:factors-->

- **Lifting capacity and span**: larger spans or special designs significantly increase the cost.
- **Hoisting mechanism type**: standard CD/MD electric hoists cost less, while European-standard wire rope hoists or open winches cost more.
- **Working environment requirements**: explosion-proof designs, anti-corrosion coatings, or adaptations for high temperature / humid environments add extra cost.
- **Control system configuration**: variable frequency drives, wireless remote control, intelligent monitoring and similar features raise the overall price.
- **Custom parameter advice**: before requesting a quotation, give the manufacturer your key parameters — capacity, span, lifting height, duty class and operating environment — so you receive an accurate quote.

## Type comparison <!--block:compare-->

Depending on building structure, headroom, duty cycle and budget, 5 ton overhead cranes can be broken down into several typical configurations. Here is a comparison of the main ones:

**Standard single girder (LD type) vs low headroom single girder (LDC type)**: the standard LD single girder crane fits ordinary workshops but needs enough vertical clearance for the main girder and hoist to travel; the low headroom crane (LDC type) mounts the hoist offset or running beside the girder, cutting the overall equipment height — for the same lifting height it saves building height, making it ideal for headroom-limited plants and retrofit projects in older buildings.

**Standard single girder (LD type) vs underhung single girder (LX type)**: the standard single girder crane is top-running and relies on floor columns or side rails for its runway, fitting regular workshops with load-bearing structures; the underhung crane (LX type) mounts its tracks under the roof or steel structure beams with the trolley suspended below, saving floor space — ideal for column-free buildings or cross-bay handling needs.

**Traditional design vs FEM European-standard design**: traditional single girder cranes mostly use a separate motor + gearbox arrangement — heavier overall and maintenance-intensive, fine for routine handling; FEM-standard single girder cranes use a three-in-one drive (motor + reducer + brake integrated), giving a compact structure, smooth starts and stops, smaller rear blind area, maintenance-free braking and high-frequency control — greatly reducing running noise and long-term maintenance cost.
