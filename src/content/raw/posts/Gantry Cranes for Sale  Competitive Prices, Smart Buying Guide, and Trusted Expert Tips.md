In the actual market, gantry crane prices typically range from a few thousand dollars for small portable gantry cranes to several hundred thousand dollars for large double girder gantry cranes. ### Single Girder Gantry Crane Price
The single girder gantry crane is a rail-mounted medium and small lifting device, mainly composed of a gantry frame (including the main beam, legs, and lower crossbeam), lifting mechanism, traveling mechanism, and electrical control system. It uses an electric hoist as the lifting mechanism, which runs along the lower flange of the I-beam main girder during operation. The gantry structure can be either box-type or truss-type: the box structure offers good craftsmanship and easy manufacturing, while the truss structure features lighter weight and stronger wind resistance. The overall machine is lightweight, simple in structure, and easy to install and maintain, making it suitable for general loading and unloading operations in outdoor areas such as factories, mines, freight yards, and warehouses within small and medium lifting capacities.

### Double Girder Gantry Crane Price
The double girder gantry crane is mainly composed of a gantry frame, trolley, crane traveling mechanism, operator’s cabin, and electrical control system. The gantry frame can adopt either a box-type or truss-type structure, and the main beam uses a double-girder off-track design. According to user requirements, the legs can be designed in either A-type or U-type configurations. The operation mode uses an enclosed cabin equipped with an adjustable seat, an insulated floor mat, and tempered glass windows. Depending on customer needs, the cabin can also be fitted with heating and cooling air conditioning, buzzers, and intercom systems. Power supply options include a cable reel or overhead sliding line.

## Gantry Crane Cost: Key Factors Affecting Total Expense
The **gantry crane cost** is not limited to the equipment’s quoted price—it includes several related expenses. The main influencing factors are as follows:



![image](images/O1HLTlyPKiolhr91oCjphgnCHmqOMcrklkDu0A2dFxk.jpg)

### 1\. Equipment Body
The equipment itself is the primary cost component, including the main beam, legs, traveling mechanism, and lifting mechanism (electric hoist/trolley). The **gantry crane price** varies greatly depending on its specifications and structural design. Parameters such as span, lifting height, load capacity, and duty class all directly affect the final cost.

* **Lifting Capacity:** Ranges from a few tons to several hundred tons. The higher the tonnage, the greater the manufacturing cost and selling price.
* **Span and Height:** Larger spans and higher lifting heights require more steel and complex designs, increasing the price.
* **Structural Form:** Single girder cranes are generally more economical for medium and light loads, while double girder cranes offer stronger load-bearing capacity and stability, making them more expensive.

### 2\. Special Design
When a crane must operate under complex conditions, special designs and material selections are required. Environments such as high temperature, low temperature, coastal, cleanroom, or explosion-proof areas significantly increase manufacturing and design costs.

### 3\. Transportation Costs
Gantry cranes are large-scale mechanical equipment typically shipped by container. Containers are usually 20 ft or 40 ft, and some oversized parts must be shipped as bulk cargo.
Transportation costs are determined by shipping companies and freight forwarders according to freight rates, cargo volume and weight, trade terms (FOB/CIF), and container type (FCL/LCL):

* **FCL (Full Container Load):** Includes local port fees, terminal handling charges (THC), documentation fees, sealing fees, container pickup fees, reinforcement, customs declaration, manifest, VGM, and data entry. It’s ideal for bulk orders with lower unit shipping costs.
* **LCL (Less than Container Load):** Includes local port fees, customs declaration, manifest, VGM, and data entry. Suitable for small-volume shipments.
* **Bulk Cargo:** Used for oversized or heavy components, quoted based on cargo dimensions.
* **Insurance & Customs Fees:** Charged as a percentage of the cargo’s declared value.
